The global auto insurance market is valued at $950 billion (2025), projected to exceed $1.75 trillion by 2034. The ride-hailing and delivery market exceeds $200 billion annually with 18%+ CAGR. DrivX Protocol sits at the intersection of these three markets: insurance ($950B), mobility ($200B+), and gig labor (435M workers). The protocol captures value from all three by creating the infrastructure layer that connects them—a verifiable, portable driving identity owned by the driver, backed by real revenue mechanics.

1.2 What DrivX Is

DrivX is a decentralized protocol that measures safe driving behavior, stores it as a portable on-chain identity, and distributes real revenue—in WETH and USDC, not printed tokens—back to the drivers who generate the underlying value. The protocol operates at two layers:

  • Layer 1 (Universal): Any driver builds a Safety Score (0–1000) from verified trip data, stakes DVX tokens, and receives weekly revenue distributions.
  • Layer 2 (Delivery): Delivery drivers gain portable reputation, direct-hire marketplace access, and platform accountability tooling.

The system is crypto-invisible to end users. Drivers register with email, never manage keys, and see earnings in familiar currency terms. The blockchain layer provides verifiability and portability without imposing cognitive overhead.

1.3 Core Thesis: Data Ownership Through On-Chain Identity

Every industry transformed by data follows the same pattern: whoever owns the data captures the value. Google owns search behavior. Meta owns social graphs. Uber owns ride data. In every case, the person who generates the data owns nothing.

DrivX is built on the conviction that this pattern is not inevitable—it is an infrastructure problem. The reason drivers don’t own their data today is not because they don’t want to. It is because no infrastructure exists to make data ownership enforceable, portable, and monetizable simultaneously.

On-chain identity solves this. When a driver’s verified behavioral record lives on a public blockchain—owned by their wallet, gated by their consent, readable by any authorized party in under 3 seconds—the data becomes:

  • Portable: Works across every insurer, employer, and platform without bilateral integration agreements. An insurer in Brazil can verify a driver’s score the same way one in Germany does—by reading the same on-chain record.
  • Composable: Other protocols and applications can build on top of it. DeFi insurance protocols can use Safety Scores for underwriting. Fleet management tools can query it. City planning systems can aggregate it. The identity becomes a primitive, not a product.
  • Revenue-generating: Every party that accesses the data pays a fee. That fee flows to the driver through the Revenue Distributor—not to a middleman, not to a platform, not to a data broker.
  • Irrevocable: No platform can delete it. No company can lock it. No intermediary is needed to verify it. The driver’s 900 Safety Score and 3,000 verified trips exist on Base regardless of which app they use, which country they drive in, or which platform they work for.

This is not a feature of DrivX. It is the reason DrivX exists. The protocol is infrastructure for driver-owned behavioral identity—and the economic layer that makes that identity worth building.

1.4 Why This Matters for DeFi

For DeFi participants evaluating DVX, the thesis reduces to this: DrivX creates a new category of on-chain identity that generates recurring revenue from off-chain economic actors (insurers, businesses, governments) who have no alternative source for this data. The token captures a share of that revenue through an immutable on-chain split. As the identity network grows (more drivers, more verified trips, more data consumers), revenue grows—without inflation, without emissions, and without dependency on speculative trading volume alone.

The driving identity is the moat. The data is the asset. The token is the revenue-share instrument on that asset.

2. Problem Analysis

2.1 The $340 Billion Mispricing

The global auto insurance market exceeds $950 billion annually. Industry data shows that the safest 30% of drivers subsidize the riskiest 20% by an estimated $340 billion per year in mispriced premiums. This mispricing persists because insurers lack real-time behavioral data and rely on demographic proxies (age, zip code, vehicle type, credit score). The economic signal of safe driving exists—it is simply not captured, verified, or returned to the driver.

A safe driver overpays by an estimated $1,200–$3,400 per year. Across 400 million safe drivers globally, this represents $480B–$1.36T in annual value extraction from the people who keep roads safe.

2.2 Platform Exploitation is Structural

Delivery and rideshare platforms extract 20–35% commission from drivers. More critically, they set delivery time targets that mathematically require exceeding posted speed limits. Analysis of major delivery platforms shows that a significant percentage of delivery time windows are physically impossible to meet without speeding. Drivers bear the safety risk, the traffic violations, and the insurance consequences. Platforms bear none.

Uber alone has 8.8 million active drivers. DoorDash, Grab, Rappi, Swiggy, and dozens of regional platforms add tens of millions more. These drivers cannot prove systemic pressure because the data is locked inside platform systems. No public accountability mechanism exists.

2.3 Reputation Lock-In

A driver with 5,000 five-star deliveries on Platform A starts at zero on Platform B. This is artificial lock-in by design. It suppresses driver bargaining power and prevents market competition for driver labor. No equivalent of a credit score exists for driving behavior—despite driving being one of the most data-rich, measurable activities a person performs daily.

3. Protocol Design & Differentiation

3.1 Core Architecture

The protocol addresses the failures above through four interlocking subsystems:

  1. Scoring Engine: Computes a composite Safety Score from verified sensor data, stored on-chain for portability.
  2. Revenue Distribution: Collects protocol revenue from multiple sources and distributes weekly to stakers, weighted by Safety Score.

Each subsystem functions independently while creating network effects when combined. A driver benefits from scoring alone (insurance discounts); staking adds revenue; the marketplace adds earning opportunities; accountability adds collective leverage.

3.3 Why Now

Three converging factors make this protocol viable today but not two years ago:

  1. Base L2 cost structure: On-chain score updates at ~$0.01/tx make per-trip writes economically feasible. On Ethereum L1, this would cost $2–$50 per trip—impossible at scale.
  2. ERC-4337 maturity: Account abstraction enables email-based onboarding with gasless transactions. Drivers never see a seed phrase. This was not production-ready until 2024.
  3. Bankr/Doppler fair launch infrastructure: 100% supply into locked LP with fee-sharing to the creator. This eliminates the need for pre-sales, VCs, or team allocations—and provides instant revenue from block 1.

The combination of cheap on-chain writes, invisible wallet UX, and fair-launch revenue mechanics creates a window where a driver-owned protocol can bootstrap without venture capital, without inflationary emissions, and without compromising on decentralization.

4. Scoring Methodology

4.1 Data Collection

The mobile client uses device accelerometer, gyroscope, and GPS to auto-detect driving sessions. Detection triggers when velocity exceeds 15 km/h for 30 seconds. Sessions terminate after 2 minutes of stationary state. GPS coordinates are sampled at minimum 10-second intervals. A passenger detection heuristic (accelerometer pattern analysis) prompts confirmation when the user may not be the operator.

4.2 Scoring Formula

The Safety Score S ∈ [0, 1000] is a weighted composite of six behavioral factors:

S = 0.25·Fspeed + 0.20·Fbraking + 0.15·Faccel + 0.15·Fcornering + 0.15·Fphone + 0.10·Ftime

Each factor Fi ∈ [0, 1000] is computed per-trip from raw sensor data:

FactorWeightInput SignalThreshold
Speed Compliance25%GPS velocity vs. posted limits (map API)Penalty per km/h over limit
Braking20%Deceleration events from accelerometerHarsh = >0.4g longitudinal
Acceleration15%Acceleration events from accelerometerAggressive = >0.3g sustained >2s
Cornering15%Lateral g-force from gyroscopeUnsafe = >0.3g lateral
Phone Usage15%Screen-on events + motion patternsAny interaction while v > 5 km/h
Time-of-Day10%Trip start/end timestampsRisk multiplier for 00:00–05:00

4.3 Rolling Average

The aggregate Safety Score uses a rolling weighted average over the most recent 100 trips with exponential decay (decay factor λ = 0.97). This ensures recent behavior dominates while maintaining stability:

Sagg = Σi=1..N (λN-i · Si) / Σi=1..N λN-i    where N = min(trips, 100)

4.4 Verification Thresholds

A score is marked “Provisional” until the driver completes 10 verified trips totaling ≥100 km. Trip records are rejected if they contain speeds >250 km/h or GPS jumps >5 km between consecutive 10-second readings. GPS gaps >60 seconds are excluded from scoring.

5.2 DVX Token

DVX is deployed externally via Bankr Bot using the Doppler Protocol. The protocol does not own or control the token contract. Key properties:

  • Fixed supply: 100,000,000,000 DVX. No mint() function exists.
  • 100% of supply placed into a Uniswap V4 pool at deployment. No pre-allocations, no vesting, no team reserves.
  • Liquidity locked permanently by Doppler Protocol. Non-removable by any party.
  • burn() reduces total supply permanently. Supply is monotonically decreasing.

5.3 Staking Contract

A standalone contract (not part of Bankr infrastructure) with the following mechanics:

  • Minimum stake: 100 DVX.
  • Revenue accrual begins at the next weekly distribution period after staking.
  • Unstaking initiates a 7-day cooldown. Revenue accrual stops immediately on unstake request. Tokens release after cooldown.
  • Accumulated revenue never forfeits regardless of claim timing.
  • Revenue distributed in three denominations: WETH (trading fees), USDC (protocol fees), DVX (trading fees).

5.4 Revenue Distributor

The core economic contract. Receives all protocol revenue and splits it:

  • 70% to stakers, weighted by Safety Score × stake amount.
  • 20% to protocol treasury (development, audits, operations).
  • 10% to Driver Incentive Pool (funds DVX Buyback).

The 70/20/10 split is hardcoded with no admin function capable of modification. Distribution executes weekly. If accumulated revenue is below 100 USDC equivalent, distribution defers. After 4 consecutive deferrals, it executes regardless.

5.5 DVX Buyback

Funded by the 10% incentive pool allocation. Executes on a fixed 7-day schedule aligned with distributions. Uses a TWAP oracle (30-minute sampling window from the Uniswap V4 pool) to determine purchase price, preventing front-running and sandwich attacks. Purchased DVX is distributed to drivers as trip rewards (max 50 DVX per verified trip).

6.4 The Critical Distinction: Revenue vs. Emissions

Most token staking systems distribute newly minted tokens (inflationary emissions) as “rewards.” This dilutes holders and creates sell pressure. DrivX does the opposite:

  • No inflation. There is no mint function. No new DVX is ever created.
  • Real revenue. Stakers receive WETH and USDC generated from actual economic activity (trading fees, insurance fees, marketplace fees).
  • Deflationary supply. The protocol actively buys DVX from the market and burns it, reducing total supply over time.
  • Aligned incentives. Revenue share is weighted by Safety Score, meaning the protocol rewards the behavior that generates the underlying value (safe driving), not passive capital alone.

A holder with 10,000 DVX staked but a Safety Score of 0 earns nothing. A driver with 1,000 DVX and a 900 Safety Score earns a meaningful share. This is by design: the protocol rewards value creation, not speculation.

7.1 Launch Mechanics

DVX launches via Bankr Bot using the Doppler Protocol on Base. The entire fixed supply (100B tokens) is placed into a Uniswap V4 liquidity pool in a single atomic transaction. There are no pre-sales, no private rounds, no team allocations, and no vesting schedules. The team is funded exclusively from the 20% protocol development allocation distributed through the Revenue Distributor.

7.2 Fee Structure

The Uniswap V4 pool charges a 1.2% swap fee on every trade. This fee is split:

RecipientShareDenomination
DrivX Protocol (Revenue Distributor)57%WETH + DVX
Bankr36.1%WETH + DVX
Bankr Ecosystem1.9%WETH + DVX
Doppler Protocol5%WETH + DVX

7.3 Revenue-Weighted Staking

Unlike typical staking where rewards are proportional to stake alone, DrivX weights by both stake and Safety Score:

sharei = (stakei × scorei) / Σj(stakej × scorej)

This creates a direct economic incentive to drive safely. A driver with a 900 Safety Score and 1,000 DVX staked earns more than a speculator with 10,000 DVX staked but a 0 Safety Score (who earns nothing).

7.4 Deflationary Pressure

Two mechanisms reduce circulating supply over time:

  1. Buyback & Burn: A governance-configurable portion of the 20% treasury allocation purchases DVX from the open market and burns it permanently.
  2. Incentive Pool Buyback: The 10% Driver Incentive Pool continuously purchases DVX from the Uniswap V4 pool (creating buy pressure) and distributes to drivers as trip rewards.

Since no mint() function exists, total supply is monotonically decreasing. Every burn is permanent and verifiable on-chain.

7.5 Early Contributor Incentive

The first 10,000 drivers who complete ≥20 verified trips during Phase 2 receive a 500 DVX bonus. This bonus is purchased from the open market using accumulated Driver Incentive Pool funds—not minted. No bonuses distribute before revenue exists to fund them.

7.6 Team Alignment

No team member holds pre-allocated tokens, hidden wallets, or insider allocations. All team wallets are published at TGE with on-chain tracking. Team compensation comes exclusively from the 20% treasury allocation. Team members acquire DVX the same way any participant does: from the open market.

9. Privacy & Consent Layer

9.1 Data Sovereignty Model

Drivers own their data. The protocol enforces this through a layered architecture:

  • Raw GPS data: Encrypted with the driver’s key, stored in object storage. Only the driver can authorize decryption.
  • Behavioral metrics: Stored in PostgreSQL with column-level encryption. Accessible only through consent-gated API.
  • Safety Score: Stored on-chain (public by design for verifiability). Detailed breakdown requires consent.

9.2 Consent Manager (On-Chain)

The Consent Manager contract uses a bitmask system for data categories:

0x01 = Safety Score only
0x02 = Trip history
0x04 = Delivery metrics
0x08 = Insurance data

Each consent grant specifies: authorized party (hashed identifier), data categories (bitmask), duration (max 12 months), and is recorded on-chain. Revocation is immediate (within one block, ~2 seconds on Base). Expired grants auto-deny without manual revocation.

10.2 Backend Services

The backend decomposes into five services behind an API gateway with tiered rate limiting (100/1000/5000 req per 15 min for public/authenticated/business tiers):

  • Trip Verification Engine: Validates GPS integrity, detects anomalies (speed >250 km/h, GPS jumps >5 km/10s), marks unverified segments, stores encrypted GPS data.
  • Scoring Engine: Computes per-trip scores, updates rolling average, writes to Safety Registry on-chain.
  • Accountability Engine: Aggregates anonymized delivery data per platform, detects unsafe speed targets (>10% over limit across 100+ deliveries), publishes reports on-chain. Enforces k-anonymity (k≥50).
  • Marketplace Service: Matches jobs to drivers within radius, manages escrow lifecycle, handles timeout/refund logic.
  • Identity Service: Deploys AA wallets, manages KYC flow (3 attempts, 30-day window), generates verifiable credentials (30-day validity).

10.3 Mobile Client

Key technical decisions for the mobile client:

  • Drive detection: Motion coprocessor (CMMotionActivityManager / Activity Recognition API) for low-power activity classification. GPS activates only when driving is confirmed. Target: <5% battery per hour.
  • Offline-first: SQLite stores up to 50 trips locally. Sync controller batch-uploads on connectivity restoration.
  • Passenger detection: Accelerometer pattern heuristic distinguishes driver-side vs. passenger-side motion. Prompts confirmation within 10 minutes; discards unconfirmed trips after 24 hours.
  • Localization: i18n framework with EN, ES, HI, PT at launch. New languages addable without app update (OTA string bundles).

10.4 Data Model

On-chain data is minimal (scores, stakes, consent, escrow). Off-chain data (trips, GPS, PII) is stored in PostgreSQL with column-level encryption and object storage with AES-256 driver-key encryption. The two layers maintain eventual consistency with max lag of one block confirmation (~2 seconds).

11. Security

11.1 Smart Contract Security

  • No admin keys. Core contracts (Revenue Distributor, Staking, DVX Token) have no owner, no admin functions, no upgrade paths. The token contract is deployed externally by Bankr Bot—the protocol team cannot modify it.
  • Non-upgradeable. No proxy patterns, no UUPS, no delegatecall. Contracts are verified on Basescan with source code.
  • Reentrancy protection. All external calls follow checks-effects-interactions. OpenZeppelin ReentrancyGuard on all state-modifying functions.
  • Flash-loan resistance. 7-day unstaking cooldown prevents flash-loan manipulation of revenue distribution weights.
  • MEV protection. DVX Buyback uses TWAP oracle (30-minute window) rather than spot price, preventing sandwich attacks.
  • Formal testing. Foundry fuzz testing with 100+ iterations per property. 29 correctness properties defined and tested.

11.2 Infrastructure Security

  • TLS 1.3 on all endpoints. JWT sessions with 30-minute timeout.
  • Rate limiting at gateway level. IP allowlisting for business API access.
  • Database: at-rest encryption, column-level encryption for PII, read replicas for query isolation.
  • Key management via HSM/KMS. 90-day rotation for API signing keys.
  • Circuit breaker pattern for blockchain RPC failures. Dead letter queue after 5 retries.

11.3 User Security

  • Account Abstraction eliminates seed phrase risk. No private keys exposed to users.
  • Paymaster with spending limits prevents gas griefing.
  • Identity verification (government ID + selfie) gates withdrawals and data sharing, not trip recording.
  • Failed revenue claims retain balance for retry—no forfeiture under any condition.

13. Technical Roadmap: Toward Trustless Scoring

13.1 The Trust Assumption in V1

In the initial protocol version, the scoring engine runs on a centralized backend. The backend receives trip data from the mobile client, computes the Safety Score, and writes the result to the Safety Registry on-chain. This means participants must trust that:

  1. The backend computes scores honestly (no manipulation).
  2. The mobile client submits real sensor data (no GPS spoofing).
  3. The trip was performed by a real, unique human (no bots, no Sybils).

This is an acceptable trust model for launch—identical to what every telematics insurer operates today—but it is not the end state. The protocol’s long-term technical roadmap systematically eliminates each trust assumption through cryptographic verification.

13.2 Phase A: Zero-Knowledge Score Proofs

Goal: A driver proves “my Safety Score is above 800” without revealing the exact score, trip history, or any raw data.

Approach: ZK-SNARK circuits (using Noir or Circom) that take the driver’s trip history as private input and produce a proof that the computed score satisfies a threshold. The verifier contract on Base checks the proof in constant time (~200K gas) without learning anything about the underlying data.

Impact: Insurers can verify risk category (Low/Medium/High) without accessing granular trip data. This eliminates the need for the Consent Manager in simple verification flows—the proof itself is the consent-free, privacy-preserving credential. It also makes the protocol GDPR-compliant by design: no personal data is ever transmitted to the verifier.

13.3 Phase B: Verifiable Computation (Trustless Scoring)

Goal: Prove that the Safety Score was computed correctly from real sensor data, without trusting the backend.

Approach: The scoring algorithm is compiled into a zkVM program (RISC Zero or SP1). The mobile client submits encrypted trip data to a prover network. The prover executes the scoring algorithm inside the zkVM and produces a proof of correct execution. The Safety Registry contract verifies this proof before accepting the score update.

Impact: The backend is no longer trusted. Anyone can verify that a score was computed by the correct algorithm on the correct input. The protocol becomes trustless at the scoring layer—a property no telematics company or DePIN project currently offers. This is the technical moat: once scoring is verifiable, the on-chain identity becomes the only provably honest driving score in existence.

13.4 Phase C: On-Device Federated Scoring

Goal: Raw GPS and sensor data never leaves the driver’s device. Only the computed score (with proof) is transmitted.

Approach: The scoring algorithm runs locally on the mobile device. The device generates a ZK proof that the score was computed from valid sensor readings (accelerometer within physical bounds, GPS coordinates forming a plausible route, timestamps monotonically increasing). The proof is submitted to the Safety Registry. Raw data is never uploaded anywhere.

Impact: True data sovereignty. No server, no backend, no cloud storage ever sees the driver’s location history. The driver’s phone is the only system that processes their data. This is the strongest possible privacy guarantee—stronger than encryption, because the data simply does not exist outside the device.

13.5 Phase D: Sybil Resistance via Driving Proofs

Goal: Prove that a unique human performed a unique trip in a real vehicle (not GPS spoofing, not emulators, not bots farming rewards).

Approach: Multi-signal attestation combining:

  • Physics consistency: Accelerometer, gyroscope, and GPS must be mutually consistent (you cannot fake lateral g-force from a desk).
  • Environmental correlation: Cross-reference reported speed with known road speed limits and traffic patterns for that time/location.
  • Device attestation: Android SafetyNet / iOS DeviceCheck to verify the app runs on a real, non-rooted device.
  • Proof-of-unique-human: Optional integration with proof-of-personhood protocols (e.g., World ID) for high-value actions (withdrawals, bonus claims).

Impact: The protocol can scale to millions of drivers without reward farming destroying the economic model. Each verified trip represents a real human driving a real vehicle on a real road.
